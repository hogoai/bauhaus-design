A short guide to the school that changed design: its history, ideas, people and classics, plus a walking route through Dessau, where it happened.

Start the tour Dessau route

01 — Timeline

Fourteen years that changed design

From Weimar to Dessau to Berlin, and from there to the whole world.

  1. 1919

    Founded in Weimar

    Walter Gropius founds the Staatliches Bauhaus by merging an art academy with a school of applied arts.

  2. 1925

    Move to Dessau

    Under political pressure the school leaves Weimar. The industrial city of Dessau offers it a new home.

  3. 1926

    The Bauhaus Building opens

    In December, Gropius’s new school building is inaugurated, together with the Masters’ Houses nearby.

  4. 1928

    New directors

    Hannes Meyer succeeds Gropius. From 1930, Ludwig Mies van der Rohe leads the school.

  5. 1932

    From Dessau to Berlin

    The Nazi-led city council closes the school in Dessau. Mies reopens it as a private institute in Berlin.

  6. 1933

    Closure

    Under pressure from the Nazi regime, the masters dissolve the Bauhaus. Many emigrate and carry its ideas around the world.

  7. 1996

    UNESCO World Heritage

    Bauhaus sites in Weimar and Dessau become World Heritage. Bernau and further Dessau buildings follow in 2017.

  8. 2019

    The centenary

    For the 100th anniversary, the new Bauhaus Museum Dessau opens in the city centre.

02 — Ideas

Four ideas that still shape design

Art and technology

“Art and technology – a new unity” was Gropius’s motto from 1923: design for industrial production, not only for galleries.

Learning by making

Workshops for metal, weaving, furniture and printing. Students trained hand and mind together, guided by masters.

The preliminary course

Every student began with form, colour and material. Johannes Itten led it first, later László Moholy-Nagy and Josef Albers.

Form and function

“Form follows function” was coined by the American architect Louis Sullivan. The Bauhaus made clear, useful form its own.

03 — People

The people behind it

Architects, painters and designers who taught side by side.

Walter Gropius

1883–1969

Founder and first director, 1919–1928. Architect of the Bauhaus Building in Dessau.

Hannes Meyer

1889–1954

Second director, 1928–1930. Put people’s needs first, known for the motto “the people’s needs instead of luxury”.

Ludwig Mies van der Rohe

1886–1969

Last director, 1930–1933. Later shaped modern architecture in the USA.

Wassily Kandinsky

1866–1944

Painter. Taught colour and form, 1922–1933.

Paul Klee

1879–1940

Painter. Taught in Weimar and Dessau, 1921–1931.

László Moholy-Nagy

1895–1946

Led the preliminary course and the metal workshop, 1923–1928. Pioneer of photography and new media.

Marianne Brandt

1893–1983

Leading designer of the metal workshop. Her lamps and tea infusers became icons.

Gunta Stölzl

1897–1983

Head of the weaving workshop and the only female master at the Bauhaus.

04 — Classics

Classics you still see today

Many Bauhaus designs were made for industrial production. Some are still manufactured a century later.

Marcel Breuer · 1925

Club chair B3 “Wassily”

A chair of bent tubular steel, inspired by bicycle handlebars. It was later named after Wassily Kandinsky.

Marianne Brandt · 1924

Tea infuser MT49

A small tea infuser of metal and ebony, built from basic geometric forms.

Wilhelm Wagenfeld, Karl J. Jucker · 1924

The Wagenfeld lamp

A table lamp of glass and metal, one of the best-known Bauhaus objects.

Herbert Bayer · 1925

Universal alphabet

A typeface with lowercase letters only, reduced to circles and straight lines.

Josef Hartwig · 1924

Bauhaus chess set

Each piece is shaped by how it moves: the rook is a cube, the bishop a diagonal cross.

Walter Gropius · 1926

The Bauhaus Building

Workshops, studios and student rooms under one roof. The glass façade of the workshop wing is its signature.

05 — Dessau route

A walking route through Dessau

Seven stops. The first ones are within walking distance; the Kornhaus and Törten are best reached by bike or bus.

  1. 1

    Bauhaus Building

    Walter Gropius · 1925–26

    The school itself and today the seat of the Bauhaus Dessau Foundation. Look out for the glass façade of the workshop wing.

  2. 2

    Masters’ Houses

    Walter Gropius · 1925–26

    Homes of Gropius, Kandinsky, Klee and other masters, a short walk from the Bauhaus through the pine trees.

  3. 3

    Bauhaus Museum Dessau

    2019

    Opened in 2019 in the city centre. It shows the collection of the Bauhaus Dessau Foundation.

  4. 4

    Former Employment Office

    Walter Gropius · 1927–29

    A semicircular building planned so that visitors could move through it efficiently. Still used by the city today.

  5. 5

    Kornhaus

    Carl Fieger · 1929–30

    A restaurant on the banks of the Elbe with a curved glass front.

  6. 6

    Törten Estate

    Walter Gropius · 1926–28

    Gropius’s housing estate, built with rationalised construction methods. It includes the Konsum building and the Steel House.

  7. 7

    Houses with balcony access

    Hannes Meyer · 1929–30

    Hannes Meyer’s affordable housing next to Törten, a World Heritage site since 2017.
